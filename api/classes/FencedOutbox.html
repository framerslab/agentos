Class FencedOutbox

Wraps an IOutbox so that a purge of a run reaches its deliveries in flight, not only its stored rows.

  • purgeContent aborts the run's sends under way before it writes the fence, waits for them to end, then empties the run's content deliveries in the wrapped outbox, delivered ones included.
  • sendClaimed claims one delivery under the run's guard: behind the fence it scrubs the run's content first, then reads the delivery again, so a purge that landed while the dispatcher resolved its destination sends the content-free bot:purged body in its place. The send itself goes out once the guard is released.
  • Once the run is fenced, every delivery enqueued for it is stored as bot:purged with an empty payload, whatever its type (bot:done included): its destination learns only the delivery's ids and the run's.

Wrap each outbox once per process and give that one wrapper to the manager and to the OutboxDispatcher: a second wrapper of the same outbox does not see the first one's sends, so a purge through one cannot cancel a send through the other. A run's enqueues, claims, scrubs and forget run one at a time, and a send runs outside that guard: an enqueue for a run, and with it the manager's handling of the run's events (a participant's stop among them), never waits for a destination; only a purge waits for the run's sends, once it has cancelled them. A delivery is sent once at a time here: a pass that takes a delivery another pass is still sending skips it. Over an outbox that evicts whole runs (an in-memory one) the wrapper has forget too, which forgets the run's fence with its deliveries, so the manager's eviction after the retention grace goes on as it does without the wrapper.

sendClaimed

  • sendClaimed(candidate, send): Promise<boolean>

  • Claims one delivery under the run's guard and sends it once the guard is released, so no enqueue or claim for the run waits for its destination. Under the guard, behind the fence, the run's content is scrubbed first; then the delivery is read again (a purge may have emptied it since due returned it) and, while it is still pending and no other send of it is under way here, send gets the fresh row and a signal that a purge of the run aborts. True when send resolved; false when the delivery is gone, no longer pending or being sent already, or a purge cancelled the send; rejects with send's own error otherwise.

purgeContent

  • purgeContent(runId): Promise<void>

  • Fences the run's deliveries and empties its content deliveries in the wrapped outbox, delivered ones included. The run's sends under way here are aborted at once, before the fence's write, and again once the fence is written; the purge then waits for them to end before it scrubs, so when it resolves no send of the run's content is still in progress in this process. A send must therefore never wait for a purge of its own run.
